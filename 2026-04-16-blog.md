Building in public, building for good

I launched my third project today.

Tirith is an open-source CLI that tracks what you're actually spending on AI API calls. It's a transparent proxy, one import to add, one to remove - and it works with any provider you already have a key for. Everything stays local on your machine.

Seventeen hours after the release tag went live, here's where it sits:

288 installs on PyPI in the last week, 107 on npm in a day
73 unique networks cloned the source in the last fortnight - 51 of them on launch day
~14 binary downloads direct from GitHub releases
771 views on the demo video

This is the third project I've shipped this summer, and I want to use today's launch as an excuse to write down what I'm actually building toward - because the projects look scattered from the outside but they aren't.

The principles

Four things guide what I pick up and what I put down:
Service. 
Specifically, it has to solve a problem a real person has told me about or that I've watched them struggle with. If I can't name the person, the project doesn't ship.

Non-profit by default. 
Public-good projects get incorporated as charitable trusts or open-sourced outright. Commercial projects exist to fund the non-profit ones. I'd rather build something useful and free than something extractive and lucrative. Reducing technology inequality. 

Sustainability. 
I'm not interested in growth curves. I want projects that can run on small infrastructure, maintained by small teams, generating enough recurring revenue or grant funding to keep existing without me personally bankrolling them. Durable, compounding.

AI-native.
Every project I ship now is built with AI tooling at its core, both in how I build it (Claude Code, rapid prototyping, agent-driven development) and what it does (LLMs as infrastructure rather than as features). This isn't a trend I'm riding. It's the ground I'm standing on.

The projects

Bowen Public

A free legal chatbot for New Zealanders. Ask it a question about tenancy, employment, family law, or any of the everyday legal situations people hit - and it gives you a plain-language answer grounded in NZ legislation. bowenpublic.com

In the process of executing a charitable trust deed. Open-sourced as Magna so anyone can stand up a jurisdiction-specific version. This is the prototype for what I think civic AI looks like: trustworthy, free at the point of use, transparently operated, grounded in the actual law of the country it serves. 

https://github.com/joedaviesio/magna

GoBuga

gobuga.org

A grant discovery platform that scans hundreds of funding sources and matches them to the organisation looking. Built on Next.js and FastAPI, multi-bot scanning.

The hypothesis: grant funding is still discovered the way it was in 2005 - manual searches, newsletters, word of mouth - while every other part of an organisation's operations has been transformed by software. GoBuga closes that gap. Non-profits find more of the money that already exists for them. This is the commercial arm of the strategy. It funds the free work.

Tirith

The CLI I launched today. A transparent proxy for AI API spend tracking. Open source, MIT licensed, local-first.

Tirith exists because I was building the other projects and kept getting surprised by API bills. The provider dashboards lag. There's no per-call breakdown tied back to your own code. So I built one.

Now it's public infrastructure for every developer hitting an API.
